**`uns`** and **`umas`** are the **plural indefinite articles** in Brazilian Portuguese.

| Singular | Plural | Meaning |
|----------|--------|---------|
| um | uns | a / an → some / a few |
| uma | umas | a / an → some / a few |

Their gender and number must agree with the noun:

- **uns** → masculine plural
- **umas** → feminine plural

### Examples

- **uns livros** = some books
- **uns amigos** = some friends
- **umas casas** = some houses
- **umas pessoas** = some people

### Main uses

**1. Indefinite quantity (most common)**

- Comprei **uns** livros. = I bought **some** books.
- Vi **umas** crianças no parque. = I saw **some** children in the park.

**2. Approximate number**

- Esperei **uns** dez minutos. = I waited **about** ten minutes.
- Daqui a **umas** duas horas. = In about two hours.

### Compared to no article

Sometimes the article is optional:

- Comprei **livros**. = I bought books. (generic; quantity not emphasized)
- Comprei **uns livros**. = I bought **some** books. (emphasizes an indefinite quantity)

### Not the same as **alguns / algumas**

- **uns / umas** = some, an unspecified quantity (neutral, common)
- **alguns / algumas** = some, **certain** or **a number of** (slightly more specific or emphatic)

Compare:
- Trouxe **uns** documentos. = I brought some documents.
- Trouxe **alguns** documentos importantes. = I brought some (certain) important documents.

### Rule to remember

- **uns** → masculine plural nouns
- **umas** → feminine plural nouns

Examples:
- **uns carros**, **uns problemas**, **uns dias**
- **umas mesas**, **umas flores**, **umas semanas**

give me super concise explanation of difference of 'por' and 'para' in brazilian portuguese with examples


----- MESSAGE (role=assistant) -----
The simplest way to think about them is:

| **Para** | **Por** |
|----------|----------|
| **Destination / goal / recipient / deadline** | **Reason / cause / means / exchange / duration / movement through** |
| **toward** | **because of / by / through / for (in exchange for)** |

### **PARA** = destination, purpose, recipient

- Vou **para** o Brasil. → I'm going **to** Brazil.
- Este presente é **para** você. → This gift is **for** you.
- Estudo **para** aprender. → I study **to** learn.
- É **para** amanhã. → It's **for** tomorrow.

---

### **POR** = cause, means, exchange, duration, path

- Obrigado **pela** ajuda. → Thanks **for** the help. (reason)
- Viajei **de** carro **por** São Paulo. → I travelled **through** São Paulo. (path)
- Paguei R$50 **por** isso. → I paid R$50 **for** it. (exchange)
- Trabalhei **por** duas horas. → I worked **for** two hours. (duration)
- O livro foi escrito **por** Machado de Assis. → The book was written **by** Machado de Assis. (agent)

---

## Quick test

If you can replace **for** with:

- **to / toward / intended for** → **para**
  - This is **for** you. → É **para** você.

- **because of / by / through / in exchange for / during** → **por**
  - Thanks **for** your help. → Obrigado **pela** ajuda.
  - I paid **for** it. → Paguei **por** isso.

### One-line memory

- **PARA = where/whom/what goal?**
- **POR = why/how/by what means/through what?**

why 'ponte' is feminina word?


----- MESSAGE (role=assistant) -----
The short answer is: **there is no semantic reason.** It's a matter of the word's historical development.

**Ponte** is feminine because it comes from the Latin noun **_pons, pontis_**, which became feminine in the Romance languages that evolved into Portuguese.

Compare:

| Language | Word | Gender |
|---------|------|--------|
| Portuguese | **a ponte** | Feminine |
| Spanish | **el puente** | Masculine |
| Italian | **il ponte** | Masculine |
| French | **le pont** | Masculine |

This shows that **gender can change over time** as languages evolve. Portuguese happened to preserve or develop the feminine gender for this word, while Spanish, Italian, and French settled on the masculine.

### Is there a rule?

No. The ending **-e** does **not** determine gender.

Examples:

- **a ponte** (feminine)
- **a noite** (feminine)
- **a parede** (feminine)
- **o dente** (masculine)
- **o leite** (masculine)
- **o pente** (masculine)

For nouns ending in **-e**, the gender usually has to be learned individually.

### A useful observation

Many Portuguese nouns ending in **-agem**, **-ção**, **-dade**, **-tude**, **-ice** are almost always feminine, but **-e** is **not** a reliable indicator of gender.

So **a ponte** is simply one of many **-e** nouns whose gender is fixed by historical usage rather than by a productive grammar rule.


----- MESSAGE (role=user) -----
Ela foi pro médico pela manhã. -> correct sentence? if so, how to translate this properly?


----- MESSAGE (role=assistant) -----
Yes, it is **completely correct** in Brazilian Portuguese.

> **Ela foi pro médico pela manhã.**

Breakdown:

- **Ela** = She
- **foi** = went
- **pro** = **para o** ("to the")
- **médico** = doctor (or doctor's office, depending on context)
- **pela manhã** = **in the morning** (literally: *por + a manhã*)

### Natural English translations

1. **She went to the doctor in the morning.** ✅ (most natural)
2. **She went to the doctor's in the morning.** ✅ (also natural, especially in British English)
3. **She went to see the doctor in the morning.** ✅ (makes the purpose explicit)

### Why **pro** but **pela**?

- **pro** = **para + o** → destination
  - Ela foi **pro** médico. = She went **to** the doctor.

- **pela** = **por + a** → time period ("during")
  - **pela manhã** = **in the morning** (literally, "during the morning")

This sentence nicely illustrates the core distinction:

- **para** → destination: **foi pro médico** ("went to the doctor")
- **por** → time period: **pela manhã** ("during the morning" → "in the morning")
