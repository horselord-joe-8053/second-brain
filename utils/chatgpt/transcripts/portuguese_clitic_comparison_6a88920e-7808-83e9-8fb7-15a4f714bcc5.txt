All **three are correct**. Here, **o / os / nos** are **direct-object clitic pronouns**.

| Sentence | Clitic | Meaning | Direct object |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Eles o viram.** | **o** | They saw **him/it**. | masc. singular |
| **Eles os viram.** | **os** | They saw **them**. | masc. plural |
| **Eles nos viram.** | **nos** | They saw **us**. | 1st-person plural |

The key is that **ver** is transitive direct:

> ver **alguém/algo** → no preposition

So:

**Eles viram João.** → **Eles o viram.**  
**Eles viram João e Pedro.** → **Eles os viram.**  
**Eles viram a nós.** → **Eles nos viram.**

For learning the whole system, the core **direct-object clitics** are:

| Person | Singular | Plural |
|---|---|---|
| 1st | **me** | **nos** |
| 2nd | **te** | **vos** *(rare in everyday BR-PT)* |
| 3rd | **o / a** | **os / as** |

So you can get:

**Ele me viu.** — He saw me.  
**Eu te vi.** — I saw you.  
**Eles o viram.** — They saw him.  
**Eles a viram.** — They saw her.  
**Eles nos viram.** — They saw us.  
**Eu os vi.** — I saw them (masc.).  
**Eu as vi.** — I saw them (fem.).

One important point for your clitic study: **me, te, nos, vos can be either direct OR indirect objects**, depending on the verb. **o/a/os/as**, however, are specifically direct-object forms.

No. **Enclisis** (placing the clitic after the verb) is **not limited to the infinitive**.

But **vê-lo** specifically is the infinitive **ver + o**:

> **ver + o → vê-lo**  
> **Quero vê-lo.** = I want to see him/it.

The spelling changes because when **o/a/os/as** follow a verb ending in **-r, -s, or -z**, that final consonant disappears and the clitic becomes **-lo/-la/-los/-las**:

| Original | + clitic | Result |
|---|---|---|
| ver + o | → | **vê-lo** |
| ver + a | → | **vê-la** |
| ver + os | → | **vê-los** |
| ver + as | → | **vê-las** |
| ajudar + o | → | **ajudá-lo** |
| fazer + o | → | **fazê-lo** |

But enclisis can occur with **conjugated verbs** too:

> **Mostre-me o livro.** — Show me the book.  
> **Diga-me a verdade.** — Tell me the truth.  
> **Ajude-me.** — Help me.

So distinguish these two ideas:

**Enclisis:** clitic comes **after any suitable verb** → *mostre-me, diga-me, vê-lo*

**-lo/-la/-los/-las transformation:** specifically occurs with **o/a/os/as** after verb forms ending in **-r, -s, -z** → *ver + o → vê-lo*.

| **os** | Ele **os quer**. | Ele **qué-los**. |
| **as** | Ele **as quer**. | Ele **qué-las**. |

That's the clean table you were looking for.

The pairs have the **same grammatical object and essentially the same meaning**:

> **Ele me quer. ↔ Ele quer-me.**  
> **Ele o quer. ↔ Ele qué-lo.**

And it makes the morphological difference immediately visible:

**me / te / nos** → verb unchanged  
> quer-me, quer-te, quer-nos

**o / a / os / as** → final **-r disappears** + **l-**
> quer + o → **qué-lo**  
> quer + a → **qué-la**  
> quer + os → **qué-los**  
> quer + as → **qué-las**

One caveat: the **enclitic forms with *quer***, especially **qué-lo/qué-la**, are formal and uncommon in everyday Brazilian Portuguese. But **as a grammar table demonstrating the rule, this is unusually clean and symmetric**.

For these three forms, the standard grammatical terminology in Brazilian Portuguese is:

| Sentence | Form of **me/mim** | Clitic placement / construction | Portuguese term |
|---|---|---|---|
| **O filme me encanta.** | **pronome pessoal oblíquo átono** | before verb | **próclise** |

**1. Próclise**
> O filme **me encanta**.  
> **me** = *pronome pessoal oblíquo átono*

**2. Ênclise**
> O filme **encanta-me**.  
> **me** = *pronome pessoal oblíquo átono*

**3. Pronome oblíquo tônico (preposicionado)**
> O filme encanta **a mim**.  
> **mim** = *pronome pessoal oblíquo tônico*

The important conceptual distinction is that **próclise and ênclise are names for the placement of an _unstressed clitic_ (pronome oblíquo átono)**. There isn't an equivalent term such as “proclisis” for **a mim**, because **mim** is a **stressed/tonic pronoun**, not a clitic.

A very useful hierarchy is:

> **pronomes pessoais oblíquos**  
> → **átonos**: *me, te, se, o, a, lhe, nos, os, as, lhes...*  
> → can undergo **próclise, ênclise** (and **mesóclise**)  
>
> → **tônicos**: *mim, ti, ele, ela, nós, eles, elas...*  
> → normally occur with a **preposition**: *a mim, para ele, de nós, com ela...*

'tonic pronoun' - 'tonic' here means?


----- MESSAGE (role=assistant) -----
Here **“tonic” means stressed** — the pronoun carries its own phonological stress (*tônico* in Portuguese).

Compare:

| Type | Examples | Stress | Can stand after a preposition? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **átono** = unstressed | **me, te, o, a, lhe, nos...** | ❌ no independent stress | generally no |
| **tônico** = stressed | **mim, ti, ele, ela, nós...** | ✅ has independent stress | ✅ yes |

For example:

> O filme **me** encanta.  
> **me** is unstressed and phonologically depends on the verb → **pronome oblíquo átono**.

versus:

> O filme encanta **a MIM**.  
> **mim** can receive clear emphasis/stress → **pronome oblíquo tônico**.

You can especially hear it in a contrast:

> Não encanta **a ELE**; encanta **a MIM**.

So **tônico** here is fundamentally a **phonological term**: the pronoun is **stressed**, as opposed to **átono** (“unstressed”).

This also explains why *me* behaves as a **clitic** (*me encanta / encanta-me*), whereas *mim* does **not**: *mim* is an independent stressed form.

'**pronome oblíquo átono**.' - '**oblíquo**' means what?


----- MESSAGE (role=assistant) -----
**Oblíquo** here basically means **non-subject / object form** of a personal pronoun.

Portuguese traditionally divides personal pronouns into:

| Type | Function | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **pronome reto** | typically **subject** | eu, tu, ele, ela, nós… |
| **pronome oblíquo** | typically **object/complement** | me, te, mim, o, a, lhe, nos… |

For example:

> **Eu** vi João. → **eu** = *pronome reto* (subject)  
> João **me** viu. → **me** = *pronome oblíquo* (object)

Then **oblíquo** is further divided into:

- **oblíquo átono** = unstressed object pronoun → **me, te, o, a, lhe, nos, se, os, as, lhes...**
- **oblíquo tônico** = stressed form, normally after a preposition → **mim, ti, ele, ela, nós...**

So in:

> O filme **me** encanta.

**me** = **pronome pessoal oblíquo átono**

Literally:
- **pessoal** → personal
- **oblíquo** → non-subject/object form
- **átono** → unstressed

A rough English parallel is **I → me**: *I* is the subject form; *me* is the object form.

> **agradar a alguém** → **O filme lhe agrada.**

> O filme agrada **ao João**.  
> → there's **a** required by *agradar*  
> → **O filme lhe agrada.**

> O filme encanta **o João**.

> → **O filme o encanta.**

> **Eu vi ele.** ← extremely common, although traditional normative grammar prefers **Eu o vi.**

But if your goal is to **learn the standard grammatical system correctly first**, use this:

**VTD + ele → `o`**  
> *Vi João.* → **Eu o vi.**

**VTI + a ele → `lhe`**  
> *Obedeci ao João.* → **Eu lhe obedeci.**

> **`o/a` replaces an OD.**  
> **`lhe` replaces an OI introduced by `a` (in the constructions where `lhe` is licensed).**

So don't learn **lhe = him/her**.

That distinction will prevent most `o` vs. `lhe` mistakes.

### 1. VTD: noun vs. tonic personal pronoun

For an ordinary **VTD**, the neutral noun direct object normally has **no preposition**:

> **O filme encanta João.**  
> **Ela consola João.**  
> **Eu vi João.**  
> **Eu amo Maria.**

But when you deliberately express that direct object with a **tonic personal pronoun** (*mim, ti, ele, ela, nós, eles, elas*), standard grammar allows—and traditionally calls for in this construction—the **objeto direto preposicionado**:

> O filme encanta **a ele**.  
> Ela consola **a ele**.  
> Ele viu **a mim**.  
> Eu amo **a ela**.

The object **remains direct**. The `a` does **not** turn it into an indirect object. Sources explicitly list tonic personal pronouns among the environments for a prepositional direct object. citeturn0search12turn0search14

So this isn't something you need to memorize verb-by-verb:

> **VTD + ordinary noun:** `ver João`  
> **VTD + tonic personal pronoun:** `ver a ele`  
> **VTD + unstressed clitic:** `vê-lo / o ver`

### 2. Therefore, there isn't a finite special list of VTDs

This behavior is **not restricted to a special list of verbs** such as *encantar* and *consolar*. It follows from the **type of object**, particularly when the direct object is expressed by a tonic personal pronoun. citeturn0search12turn0search14

| VTD | Noun OD | Tonic-pronoun OD | Clitic OD |
|---|---|---|---|
| **ver** | Vi **João**. | Vi **a ele**. | **Eu o vi.** |
| **amar** | Amo **Maria**. | Amo **a ela**. | **Eu a amo.** |
| **consolar** | Consolei **João**. | Consolei **a ele**. | **Eu o consolei.** |

### 3. Is there an opposite phenomenon with VTI?

**Yes—and this is very important for your `lhe` question.**

Some verbs take a complement introduced by **`a`**, yet that complement **cannot necessarily be replaced by `lhe`**.

For example, normative grammar traditionally gives:

> **Eu assisti ao filme.**  
> Eu assisti **a ele**.  
> ❌ ~~Eu lhe assisti.~~ *(for “I watched it”)*

Likewise, *aspirar* meaning **“to desire/aim for”**:

> Ele aspira **ao cargo**.  
> Ele aspira **a ele**.  
> ❌ ~~Ele lhe aspira.~~

Why? Because modern grammatical analysis distinguishes:

**true OI/dative:**

> obedecer **a João** → **lhe** obedecer  
> agradar **a João** → **lhe** agradar

from other **prepositional complements**:

> assistir **ao filme** → assistir **a ele**  
> aspirar **ao cargo** → aspirar **a ele**

An `a`-prepositional complement is therefore **not automatically an OI replaceable by `lhe`**. One grammatical diagnostic for a true indirect object is precisely whether it permits **lhe/lhes** substitution. citeturn0search2

### This gives you a much safer hierarchy

Don't use:

> ❌ **“I see `a` → use `lhe`.”**

Instead:

**First determine the grammatical function:**

> **OD** → `o / a / os / as`  
> **OI/dative** → `lhe / lhes`  
> **other prepositional complement** → usually keep the preposition + tonic pronoun (*a ele, dele, com ele,* etc.)

And there's a neat symmetry behind the confusion:

| What you see | Could be |
|---|---|
| **João** | OD: *Vi João.* |
| **a João** | OI **or** another prepositional complement |
| **a ele** | **OD preposicionado**, OI, **or** another *a*-prepositional complement |
| **o** | unambiguously OD |
| **lhe** | unambiguously OI/dative in the standard system |

So for **choosing `o` vs. `lhe`**, the truly safest learner strategy is to learn the **regência + grammatical function** of each verb/construction: *ver alguém → OD*, *obedecer a alguém → OI*, *assistir a algo → complemento preposicionado* (in the traditional “watch” construction), rather than relying on the mere presence or absence of **a**. citeturn0search9turn0search11

## 🟨 Practical `lhe` test for a BR-PT learner

Test the verb using:

> **Subject + Verb + `a` + explicit object name/noun**  
> **NOT a pronoun, and conceptually separate any leading article**

Examples of the test form:

> **O aluno obedece a João.**  
> **O filme agrada a João.**

The purpose of using an explicit noun/name is to avoid being misled by **a ele / a ela**, because those forms can also occur with an **objeto direto preposicionado**.

### 🟢 When the test works

| Verb | Test with explicit noun | Result with `lhe` | Verdict | Why |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **obedecer** | O aluno obedece **a João**. | O aluno **lhe obedece**. | 🟢 **Works** | *a João* is a true OI/dative; **lhe** replaces it. |
| **agradar** (“please”) | O filme agrada **a João**. | O filme **lhe agrada**. | 🟢 **Works** | *a João* is OI; **lhe** is appropriate. |
| **encantar** | O filme encanta **João**. | O filme **o encanta**. | 🔴 **Fails test → no `lhe`** | *encantar alguém* is VTD; João is OD → **o**, not *lhe*. |
| **consolar** | Ela consola **João**. | Ela **o consola**. | 🔴 **Fails test → no `lhe`** | *consolar alguém* is VTD; João is OD → **o**, not *lhe*. |

So these four give you a very useful basic contrast:

> 🟢 **obedecer a João → obedecer-lhe**  
> 🟢 **agradar a João → agradar-lhe**  
>
> 🔴 **encantar João → encantá-lo / o encantar**  
> 🔴 **consolar João → consolá-lo / o consolar**

And importantly, don't use **a ele** for this diagnostic:

> *O filme encanta **a ele*** can be an emphatic **OD preposicionado**.

That would make a VTD superficially look like your `lhe` pattern.

## 🟠 Warning: false positives

Unfortunately, passing the **`a + explicit noun` test does NOT guarantee `lhe`**.

Traditional grammar calls several of these verbs VTI, but modern syntactic analysis can distinguish their complements from the dative/OI that **lhe** normally represents. In particular, *assistir* (“watch”) and *aspirar* (“aim for”) take **a** but do not permit replacement by *lhe* in the standard construction. citeturn0search1turn0search2

| Verb & meaning | Passes your `a + noun` test? | Can use `lhe`? | Correct alternative | ⚠️ |
|---|---|---:|---|---|
| **aspirar** = aim for | Ele aspira **a cargos melhores**. | ❌ | Ele aspira **a eles**. | 🟠 False positive |
| **assistir** = watch | Ele assiste **a filmes brasileiros**. | ❌ | Ele assiste **a eles**. | 🟠 False positive |
| **proceder** = carry out | Eles procederam **a investigações**. | ❌ | Eles procederam **a elas**. | 🟠 False positive |
| **atender** | ⚠️ Depends on meaning/construction | ⚠️ Variable | Must check the construction | 🟠 Don't use as a simple diagnostic |

*Proceder a* is a particularly clear example: *proceder ao inventário* is traditionally called transitivo indireto, but its complement cannot be replaced by *lhe* in the relevant sense; modern analysis therefore treats it as a prepositional/oblique complement. citeturn0search7

**Assistir** also shows why meaning matters. *Assistir a um filme* (“watch”) and *assistir alguém* (“assist/help”) have different regencies. citeturn0search0turn0search9

### 🧠 The rule I'd put in your study notes

Your test is excellent as a **filter**, not as proof:

> ### `a + explicit noun` test
>
> 🔴 **Doesn't pass** → normally **NO `lhe`**.  
> 🟢 **Passes** → **`lhe` is a candidate**, but not guaranteed.
>
> Then ask:
>
> **Can this `a + noun` complement actually be replaced by `lhe/lhes`?**
>
> If **yes** → true OI/dative → **lhe/lhes**.  
> If **no** → prepositional/oblique complement → keep **a + tonic form**.

The classic contrast to memorize is therefore:

> **obedecer a João → obedecer-lhe** ✅  
> **agradar a João → agradar-lhe** ✅  
>
> **assistir a filmes → ~~assistir-lhes~~** ❌  
> **aspirar a cargos → ~~aspirar-lhes~~** ❌  
> **proceder a investigações → ~~proceder-lhes~~** ❌

This distinction is supported by grammatical analyses that specifically use **substitution by `lhe`** as a diagnostic separating a true indirect object from an *a*-introduced oblique/prepositional complement. citeturn0search15

### 🧠 The pattern worth memorizing

The four clean examples:

> **obedecer a João** → **obedecer-lhe** ✅  
> **agradar a João** → **agradar-lhe** ✅  
>
> **encantar João** → **encantá-lo / encanta-o** ✅, not *encantar-lhe*  
> **consolar João** → **consolá-lo / consola-o** ✅, not *consolar-lhe*

Then memorize the main **false-positive warning**:

> **aspirar a X** → **a ele**, NOT *-lhe*  
> **assistir a X** (“watch”) → **a ele**, NOT *-lhe*  
> **proceder a X** → **a ele**, NOT *-lhe*

So your test is still a useful first filter:

**No required `a` → 🔴 don't use `lhe`.**  
**Required `a` → 🟢 `lhe` is possible, but check for exceptions.**

And **aspirar, assistir (watch), proceder** are good exceptions to put in a box immediately underneath the rule. *Atender* is better marked **“regency varies; check the construction”** rather than memorized as an absolute exception. citeturn0search4turn0search7

Correct — **❌ A polícia as procedeu** is not correct for “The police carried out the investigations.”

This is precisely what makes **proceder a** interesting.

> **A polícia procedeu às investigações.** ✅  
> **A polícia procedeu a elas.** ✅  
> **A polícia as procedeu.** ❌  
> **A polícia procedeu-as.** ❌  
> **A polícia lhes procedeu.** ❌  
> **A polícia procedeu-lhes.** ❌

Why? In **proceder a algo** = “carry out / undertake something”, the complement is **prepositional**:

> proceder **a** + investigações

It is **not a direct object**, so you cannot replace it with **as**.

And it is not the kind of indirect/dative object that can be replaced with **lhes**, either.

Therefore it must retain its preposition:

> **às investigações → a elas**

This gives you three useful categories:

| Construction | Example | Pronoun replacement |
|---|---|---|
| **OD** | consolar **as meninas** | **consolá-las / as consolar** |
| **OI/dative** | obedecer **às professoras** | **obedecer-lhes / lhes obedecer** |
| **Prepositional complement** | proceder **às investigações** | **proceder a elas** — no clitic equivalent |

This third category is the missing piece that explains why **“VTI → lhe” is not universally true**.
