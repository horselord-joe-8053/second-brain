For **Brazilian Portuguese**, the easiest way to think about contractions is this:

> **There are only 4 truly productive prepositions that form standard contractions:**
>
> - **de**
> - **em**
> - **a**
> - **por**
>
> Everything else is either:
> - **never contracted** (e.g. com, sem, para in formal writing), or
> - **colloquial only** (e.g. pra, pro, num in many contexts). citeturn0search5turn0search0

Below is a systematic reference.

---

# 1. Definite article (o/a/os/as)

These are **100% mandatory** in standard Brazilian Portuguese.

| Preposition | o | a | os | as | Formal? |
|------------|----|----|----|----|----------|
| de | do | da | dos | das | ✅ Always |
| em | no | na | nos | nas | ✅ Always |
| a | ao | à | aos | às | ✅ Always |
| por | pelo | pela | pelos | pelas | ✅ Always |
| para | pro | pra | pros | pras | ❌ Informal only |

Examples

- do Brasil
- na escola
- ao médico
- pela manhã
- pra casa (informal)

The last row (**pro/pra**) is extremely common in speech, but is normally avoided in formal writing. citeturn0search2turn0search5

This is where Brazilian Portuguese differs from European Portuguese.

| Full | Contraction | Brazil |

## Rule of thumb (95% accurate for Brazilian Portuguese)

| If you see... | Use |
|---------------|-----|
| **de + o/a/os/as** | **Always contract** (do, da, dos, das) |
| **em + o/a/os/as** | **Always contract** (no, na, nos, nas) |
| **a + o/a/os/as** | **Always contract** (ao, à, aos, às) |
| **por + o/a/os/as** | **Always contract** (pelo, pela, pelos, pelas) |
| **de/em + ele/ela/eles/elas** | **Always contract** (dele, nele, etc.) |
| **de/em/a + demonstratives** | **Always contract** (deste, nesse, àquele, etc.) |
| **de/em + um/uma** | Prefer full form in formal Brazilian writing; contractions are common in speech |
| **para + anything** | Use full form in formal writing; **pra/pro** only in informal speech/writing |
| **Any other preposition** | Do **not** contract |

## The three groups worth remembering

### 1. Always standard (safe everywhere)

These can be used in **every register**:

- do, da, dos, das
- no, na, nos, nas
- ao, à, aos, às
- pelo, pela, pelos, pelas
- dele, dela, deles, delas
- nele, nela, neles, nelas
- deste, nesse, naquele, etc.
- àquele, àquela, etc.
- daqui, daí, dali

---

### 2. Brazilian informal but extremely common

- pra, pro, pras, pros
- num, numa
- dum, duma

These are perfectly natural in conversation, messages, social media and informal writing, but are usually avoided in academic, legal and other highly formal writing.

---

### 3. Rare in modern Brazilian Portuguese

- duns, dumas
- nuns, numas
- prum, pruma (much less common than **pra um / pra uma**)

These are grammatically possible but uncommon enough that many Brazilians rarely use them naturally. In everyday Brazilian Portuguese, speakers typically prefer:

- **de uns** instead of **duns**
- **em uns** instead of **nuns**
- **pra um** instead of **prum**
- **pra uma** instead of **pruma**

In **modern Brazilian Portuguese**:

- **num/numa** are **extremely common** in speech across all education levels.

✅ Everyday

> Moro **num** apartamento.

✅ Formal speech (lecture, interview)

> Moro **num** apartamento.

Preferred in formal writing

> Moro **em um** apartamento.

| Form | Speech | Formal writing |
|------|---------|----------------|
| **pra/pro** | Extremely common | Generally avoided |

## Essential corrections to the earlier table

### 1. **Num/numa are fully standard**

These are all correct, including formal writing:

- **num documento oficial**
- **numa decisão judicial**
- **nuns casos específicos**
- **numas circunstâncias excepcionais**

Using **em um/em uma** is also correct in Brazilian Portuguese. The choice may depend on rhythm, emphasis or avoidance of ambiguity—not simply formality. Brazilian official-writing materials repeatedly use *num* and *numa*. citeturn953543search2turn998258search1turn998258search3turn998258search7turn998258search17

### 2. **Dum/duma are also standard, but less frequent in Brazil**

Both are grammatically valid:

- **O resultado depende dum cálculo complexo.**
- **Trata-se duma situação incomum.**

Nevertheless, Brazilians frequently prefer:

- **de um cálculo**
- **de uma situação**

So their relative rarity is a matter of **Brazilian usage preference**, not grammatical incorrectness. The same applies to **duns/dumas**. citeturn953543search0turn953543search10

### 3. **Nuns/numas are not rare grammatical forms**

Their frequency is naturally lower because **uns/umas** occurs less often than **um/uma**, but the contractions themselves are completely regular and standard:

- **Ele voltou nuns dias muito movimentados.**
- **Isso aconteceu numas cidades do interior.**

### 4. **Pra is not exclusively _para + a_**

This is especially important:

- **pra casa** may correspond to **para casa** or **para a casa**, depending on the syntax.
- **pra você** = **para você**
- **pra fazer isso** = **para fazer isso**
- **pro médico** = **para o médico**

Therefore, **pra** is first of all an informal reduction of the preposition **para**; in certain constructions, it simultaneously absorbs the feminine article **a**. **Pro/pros/pras** visibly include an article. citeturn953543search21turn953543search14

## One important exception: do not contract before an infinitive subject

Normally:

> Gostei **da apresentação da Ana**.

But when **a Ana** is the subject of an infinitive, careful formal writing separates the elements:

> Está na hora **de a Ana apresentar** o projeto.  
> Apesar **de ele conhecer** o problema, não respondeu.

Not:

> Está na hora **da Ana apresentar** o projeto.

The separation shows that **a Ana** or **ele** belongs syntactically to the infinitive, rather than to the preceding preposition. citeturn953543search5turn953543search19
