# Technician knowledge handover — GE / GE Profile bottom-freezer refrigerators

**Knowledge source:** *REFRIGERATORS — Bottom Freezer: OWNER’S MANUAL & INSTALLATION INSTRUCTIONS* (GE and GE Profile; 18, 19, 21, 22, 24, 25, 26, 27 cu. ft.), publication 49-1000472 Rev. 9, 04-26. **Authorized source:** this PDF alone, English printed pages 1–52. **Evidence categories:** `[DOCUMENTED]` and `[UNKNOWN]`. **Important:** the folder identifier `GNE27JYMFS` is not evidence that every illustrated option is fitted to that exact suffix. The manual covers many related configurations. **No external appliance information was used.**

## How the support agent should use this handbook

1. Ask for the exact model identifier from the label **on the left side near the middle of the refrigerator compartment** (p. 1), and ask what physical control arrangement, dispenser, icemaker, and freezer layout is present. Never assume a feature just because it appears in this family manual.
2. Search for an applicable `[DOCUMENTED]` unit and follow its procedure, including prerequisites, time allowances and safety limitations. Use an `[UNKNOWN]` unit only for the part the manual cannot establish; do not let an inapplicable UNKNOWN unit replace a valid instruction for another model.
3. Do not attempt or recommend electrical, refrigerant-system or unspecified mechanical repairs. The manual says repairs are for qualified service professionals (pp. 3–5, 52).
4. The customer has the original PDF. Where useful, tell them the printed **page, heading and actual figure letter**; no diagrams are reproduced here. Page numbers mean English printed pages, not arbitrary viewer offsets.
5. Customer-question wording below is an aid to search, **not** evidence that an unstated failure mode exists.

## Safety and escalation — GE-SAFE-001 [DOCUMENTED; pp. 3–5, 13–15, 20, 24, 38, 41–43]

- **Electrical:** properly install and ground the appliance. Use its own grounded three-prong, 115 V AC, 60 Hz outlet on a fused 15 A or 20 A supply; never remove the ground prong or use an adapter or extension cord. A licensed electrician should correct an ungrounded two-prong outlet. Unplug by gripping the plug, never pulling the cord. Stop using a damaged power cable and seek qualified replacement. **Control-panel OFF does not electrically disconnect the unit** (pp. 3–4).
- **Before cleaning/repair:** unplug; panels and parts must be replaced before operation. Repairs by qualified service personnel. Keep gasoline, explosive aerosol propellants and flammable vapors away. Keep children away from moving and pinch-point areas. Do not allow children to play in or climb the cabinet. Do not use harsh cleaners that can damage plastic.
- **Glass / freezer / food:** do not wash cold glass shelves with warm water (thermal break risk); avoid glass impacts, wet-hand contact with frozen surfaces, touching icemaker ejector parts or heater while plugged in. The manual says not to refreeze fully thawed frozen foods; it does not supply a complete food-safety decision system.
- **Water:** potable cold supply only, **25–120 psi (172–827 kPa)** for dispenser and icemaker. Wrong water-supply conditions warrant correction before operation.
- **Refrigerant:** check appliance rating label. **Only if labeled R600a (isobutane)**, follow flammable-refrigerant restrictions: protect sealed tubing; authorized service with authorized parts only; keep ventilation openings clear; never use sharp metal picks or electrical devices to defrost or electrical appliances inside food compartments. For unwanted frost, the manual specifies a plastic or wooden scraper/spatula. Disposal of refrigerant/foam requires applicable special local procedures; never pierce the lines (p. 5).
- **Disposal:** remove refrigerator/freezer doors from a discarded unit, but leave shelves so children cannot readily climb inside. Dispose/recycle according to relevant rules (p. 4).
- **Ice dispenser:** do not put fingers or objects in the crusher discharge; use a sturdy glass (p. 13).
- **Installation:** move using a padded hand truck, side against truck; two people are strongly recommended; keep power cord/tubing safe. Be aware of the unstable/detaching handle fall hazard (pp. 24–26).

## Temperature and control selection — GE-CTRL-001 [DOCUMENTED; pp. 8–11]

**Rotary models, Figure A:** two internal knobs. Default **Colder** position; allow **24 hours** to stabilize. Approximate knob guide: refrigerator Coldest **34°F (1.1°C)**, Colder **37°F (2.8°C)**, Cold **44°F (6.7°C)**; freezer Coldest **−6°F (−21.1°C)**, Colder **0°F (−17.8°C)**, Cold **6°F (−14.4°C)**. Knob position **0 = OFF**. Avoid placing food directly against an air outlet; blocked airflow can cause erratic temperatures. Never assume rotary controls have the electronic button functions below.

**Top-control models, Figure B:** factory refrigerator **37°F (2.8°C)**, freezer **0°F (−17.8°C)**. Wait **24 hours** after adjustment. To enter refrigerator-control OFF, simultaneously touch **Freezer + Fridge** for **3 seconds**; this is not unplugging. **Door Alarm** sounds after a door is open more than **2 minutes**, stops on closure. To change °F/°C, hold **Door Alarm + Freezer** for **5 seconds**. **TurboCool** rapidly cools the refrigerator compartment; on depicted top panels hold **Turbo Cool** for **3 seconds**, `tc` appears. Temperature of fridge cannot be adjusted while TurboCool is active, and the freezer setting is unaffected; refrigerator fans may continue when a door is opened. Recommended occasions in the manual: large loads, warm leftovers, food left out at room temperature, extended outage. **Water Filter** button held **3 seconds** resets the filter indicator on that variant; **Filter Status** displays filter life percentage on another. Determine which button is present rather than interchanging them.

**External-control dispenser models, Figure C:** same factory temperature setpoints and 24-hour stabilization. Press **Alarm** to toggle door alarm (more than 2 minutes). Hold **Lock** **3 seconds** to lock or unlock ice, water and other controls; **Light** toggles dispenser LED light. The pictured panel starts **TurboCool by holding Alarm for 3 seconds**, displaying `tc` (distinct from top-control TurboCool button). Hold **Freezer + Alarm** for **5 seconds** to change °F/°C. The OFF method is **Freezer + Fridge** held **3 seconds**, but does not disconnect power. On filter variants, **Water Filter** reset requires a **3-second hold**; **Filter Status** displays remaining percentage. Remove protective film from controls if still present from installation.

**Customer phrasings:** “Why does `tc` appear?”; “Door alarm keeps beeping”; “How do I change Fahrenheit?”; “Why can't I change refrigerator temperature?”; “Panel is locked”; “Is OFF safe before working inside?” — use the relevant panel's instructions, not a generic sequence.

**Manual figure references:** Figure A (p. 8) for knobs; Figure B (pp. 8–9) for top panels; Figure C (pp. 10–11) for external controls.

## Sabbath Mode — GE-SABBATH-001 [DOCUMENTED; p. 12]

Sabbath Mode is on **some models**. It turns off temperature-control interaction and advanced features such as the door alarm; interior lighting may turn off or remain dim regardless of door state; the compressor uses timed defrost. **External control panel:** hold **Lock + Light** simultaneously **5 seconds** to enter or exit. **Top control panel:** depending on fitted variant, hold **Alarm + Water Filter** or **Alarm + Filter Status** simultaneously **5 seconds** to enter or exit. The **icemaker must be manually switched OFF** while in Sabbath Mode; return switch ON afterward if wanted. Locations of the different icemaker switches appear in **Figures D and E**, p. 12. Do not touch mechanism while powered. The manual **does not establish** a Sabbath Mode key sequence for rotary-knob GBE21A/D (see `UNKNOWN-SABBATH-ROTARY`).

## Dispenser operation, safe handling and problems — GE-WATER-001 [DOCUMENTED; p. 13]

**External water/ice dispenser (if installed):** place sturdy glass against cradle/paddle. Spill shelf **does not self-drain** and must be wiped. New installation may contain air; hold paddle **at least 5 minutes** to purge air/fill system, then **discard first six full glasses**. Keep fingers out of crusher opening. Don't add bagged/tray ice to the door icemaker bucket; foreign ice may jam. Avoid narrow glasses/overfilling, and keep glass near (not touching) opening. If chute ice jams, the manual permits removing bucket and using a **wooden spoon** to poke through ice, rather than fingers or sharp objects. Occasional crushed pieces when Cubed selected, water drips after crushed ice, and light frost mound at chute door after repeated crushing are described as normal.

**Internal water dispenser (if installed):** located at **left interior wall**. Hold glass in recess, push button; after release, keep glass beneath spout **2–3 seconds** because residual water may flow. At first installation purge with button for **at least 5 minutes** and throw away first **six glasses**. Wipe dispenser periodically with a clean cloth/sponge.

**Door ice bucket, some models:** open left refrigerator door, release bin-door latch, lift bucket using handhold clear of its bottom locators. To reinstall, put it on guide brackets and push until seated. If it does not seat, manual says rotate **ice-bucket fork clockwise ¼ turn**. Refer to unlabeled *Door Ice Bin* illustration, p. 13. Never cool beverage cans/food in door icemaker bin; objects may jam auger.

## XWFE water filter — GE-FILTER-001 [DOCUMENTED; pp. 14–15, 47, 49–50]

Applies to **XWFE-equipped models**; verify actual installed filter and whether the interface has a filter indicator. Holder is near the **back, upper-right corner** of refrigerator compartment. Some models use RFID to detect filter/leaks/status. The manual identifies FCC ID `ZKJ-294D2018`, IC `10229A-294D2018`; not every model is said to include this electronics.

**When to replace:** red filter indicator blinking (on external-panel models while dispensing; on top-panel models while door open) means replacement is soon needed. Without indicator, replace at least **every six months** or sooner if dispenser/icemaker flow decreases. Rated capacity **170 gal (643.5 L)**, rated service flow **0.5 gpm (1.89 L/min)**, potable supply, **25–120 psi**, water temperature **33–100°F (0.6–38°C)**. The manual cautions that non-GE-branded filters have not been qualified by GE; it does *not* establish that every third-party cartridge is inherently incompatible.

**Remove:** (1) Squeeze two front housing tabs, lower cover. (2) Rotate filter down. (3) Grasp filter and slowly turn **counterclockwise about ¼ turn** until it releases. Some dripping is possible. **Caution:** trapped air can eject cartridge.

**Install:** (1) Open housing. (2) Align ports and insert. (3) Turn **clockwise about 90°** until arrow on filter meets arrow on housing, without overtightening. (4) Push filter into clips. (5) Push housing lid upward to latch. (6) If **dispenser-only**, run **2 gal (7.6 L)** or about **5 minutes**; spurting/air bubbles may occur; if no water, check full right rotation. If **icemaker-only**, discard the **first bin of ice**; initial droplets or discoloration can occur during purge. (7) Where relevant hold **RESET WATER FILTER** for **3 seconds**; some models detect new filter automatically.

**Bypass plug:** if cartridge is absent, GE says **you MUST fit bypass plug** to reduce risk of leaks. Dispenser and icemaker won't work without either cartridge or bypass. Bypass installs like filter. To obtain free plug, manual gives US **833.4BODEWELL**, Canada **800.561.3344** (p. 14). Filter ordering: `gewaterfilters.com`, US 877.959.8688; Canada 800.661.1616 (p. 15).

**Important distinct scenarios:** First-ever water installation is a **5-minute purge plus discard six glasses** (p. 13); newly installed cartridge is **2 gal/approx. 5 minutes** for dispenser-only (p. 14). The troubleshooting table also lists a **3-minute/approximately 1½ gal** run for **spurting** from a newly installed filter (p. 46). These are different stated contexts; do not silently replace one with another.

**Filter trouble:** red light remains after replacement—verify **XWFE, not XWF**, correct fit, filter-label scratches/damage, indicator reset if appropriate (p. 47). `LEAK` message, red flashing status, buzzer, disabled water—filter leak detected: replace filter and wipe water from its area. `Err`, red flashing, buzzer, disabled water—filter not detected or no communication: verify XWFE and intact label, replace if damaged. Beeping when paddle pressed may indicate filter not properly fitted or filter leak; check mounting (p. 47). The source does not describe internal RFID electrical tests.

**Original PDF reference:** the unlabeled installation picture beneath *INSTALLING THE FILTER IN A BOTTOM FREEZER REFRIGERATOR*, printed p. 14, illustrates location/orientation. Performance Data Sheet, p. 50, has extensive contaminant-specific test results; do not confuse certified percentage reduction with a guarantee of local water quality. Examples on p. 50 include particulate, chlorine, cyst, lead, mercury, VOCs and pharmaceuticals; quote a contaminant-specific value only after checking its particular source row.

## Storage, shelves and freezer — GE-STORAGE-001 [DOCUMENTED; pp. 16–19]

- **Refrigerator shelf removal:** unload; lift front, lift rear and draw shelf out. Reinstall by inserting rear top hook into track slot with shelf tilted, lower front until lower catch locks. **QuickSpace:** front half slides under rear half to clear tall items; rear half is not adjustable. (p. 16)
- **Adjustable and nonadjustable door bins:** lift straight up, pull away; reinstall on molded supports and push down to lock. Applicability varies. (p. 16)
- **Climate Zone produce pans:** hold fruit/vegetable items separately; wipe collected water below. Pull to stop, lift over stop and draw forward. Full-width pan: pull to stop, raise front out; replace rear first then lower front into slides. Divider: at full extension slide to right to release; reverse to reinstall. Movable snack pan similarly lifts out at stop. (p. 17)
- **Freezer drawer with baskets:** some have ice bucket, top full-width basket and lower basket/divider. Never load above rim: sticking/jamming risk. Top basket: pull to stop, lift off slides, replace on rails and above lower-basket slides; close fully. Lower basket: remove upper, lift lower front, pull forward to release rear pin/slot; replace with basket tilted back, seat pin and bracket tabs. (p. 18)
- **Freezer door configuration:** fixed full-width wire shelf plus sliding plastic basket. To remove basket, fully extend, lift front and pull out; replace in lower liner rails. Wire shelf **not intended to slide**; for cleaning push sideways while raising other end, and reinstall in supports. (p. 19)

## Automatic icemaker — GE-ICE-001 [DOCUMENTED; pp. 20–21]

Some models have door icemaker; others freezer-compartment icemaker; some are only icemaker-ready. Check fitted hardware. Supply potable cold water within **25–120 psi**. On fitted variants, slide switch to **OFF** until plumbing connected, then **ON**; the different switch/feeler-arm arrangements are pictured as **Figures D/E**, p. 12, and Figure D, p. 20. The manual says a new refrigerator may take **12–24 hours** to start producing ice. A described model makes approximately **seven cubes/cycle, 100–130 cubes per 24 hours**, varying by conditions. Icemaker fills at approximately **15°F (−10°C)**. Throw away first batches to flush line. Unobstructed feeler arm stops ice at full bin; a few joined cubes are normal. Old unused ice can cloud, shrink or taste stale. Low supply pressure may cause multiple fill cycles. Some ice-bucket removal methods differ; consult the *For Refrigerator Models with Icemaker in the Door* drawing on p. 20 and *For Refrigerator Models with Icemaker in the Freezer Compartment* p. 21.

**Do not operate icemaker ON with water disconnected**: manual warns frequent buzzing and damage to water valve. Do not touch ejector mechanism or heating element while powered.

## Cleaning, movement and lighting — GE-CARE-001 [DOCUMENTED; pp. 22–23]

Interior: unplug; wipe with solution of baking soda and warm water as described in source, rinse and dry; clean gaskets/liners carefully. Do not use harsh chemicals, abrasive pads, scouring powders or solvents that can damage surfaces. For outside finishes, instructions differ for fingerprint-resistant stainless, other stainless and plastic; use only the finish-specific methods on p. 22. Avoid stainless cleaner on certain fingerprint-resistant finishes; the manual provides surface compatibility tables. For behind-unit cleaning move refrigerator **straight out and back**, without crushing cord or water line; rear floor supports can be damaged by sideways motion. In long absence/vacation, remove perishable foods and follow p. 23 instructions, including turning icemaker OFF and shutting water as directed. For moving, secure loose parts and keep unit protected; lighting uses LED: when an LED fails, manual directs calling service rather than treating LEDs as user-replaceable bulb assemblies (p. 23, *Replacing the Lights*).

## Installation prerequisites — GE-INSTALL-001 [DOCUMENTED; pp. 24–26]

The manual's location restrictions: do not install where ambient falls below **60°F (16°C)** or exceeds **100°F (37°C)**; avoid water/rain and direct sun; floor must carry refrigerator when full. **Customer question: "What is the rear clearance for installation?"** The required space behind the refrigerator is **2 in (50 mm)**; at the sides allow **1/8 in (3 mm)** and above at cabinet/hinge cover **1 in (25 mm)**. Measure doors/passageways; the manual says minimum 36 in (91.4 cm) if doors stay attached during move and recommends two movers/padded truck (p. 26). **Actual model dimensions vary markedly**; use dimension letter A–I from the printed **APPLIANCE DIMENSIONS** table, p. 25, plus correct French/single-door schematic. There are seven capacity/door-type columns; DO NOT assume values from a different category. Examples: height A is generally **69 7/8 in (177.5 cm)** in this table; width B is **30, 33 or 36 in** depending on category; consult exact row and full diagrams for door swing dimensions C–I. Cabinet opening should allow baseboards, countertop overhang and desired clearances. Do not claim exact GNE27JYMFS width solely from family capacity without matching door group.

**Handles:** align slots with door mounting studs and slide down to hard stop; verify secure; freezer handle methods vary (slide-on vs set-screw), p. 27. Improper fitting can cause unexpected detachment and fall hazard. **Door/fitting changes** must follow the exact appropriate pictured variant, not a generic guide.

## Door and freezer-drawer removal / reinstallation — GE-INSTALL-DOORS-001 [DOCUMENTED; pp. 28–37]

There are separate procedures and tool/fastener patterns. **French-door removal pp. 28–29:** first isolate electric power and manage ice/water lines and top hinge coverings per instructions; right and left door procedures differ, and the left may have dispenser wiring and water connection. Keep doors supported during hinge detachment and do not damage lines. **French-door refitting pp. 30–31:** restore hinges/fasteners, electrical connectors and the water tubing to their documented positions and check door closure. Consult numbered steps and unlabeled hinge/tube illustrations under *REMOVING FRENCH DOORS* and *REINSTALLING FRENCH DOORS* on those pages before doing any physical work.

**Freezer drawer pp. 32–33:** empty drawer, remove basket and drawer front by release of specified screws/rail mechanisms; reinstall drawer front aligned to rails and latches/fasteners, then replace basket and verify closure. **Single-door reversal pp. 34–37:** hinges, handles, hardware, plug buttons and door stops are changed side-for-side in a defined order, with safety warning and diagrams. It applies only to reversible single-door configurations, **not** as an interchangeable French-door procedure. This is physical installation work requiring caution, supporting heavy doors and power disconnection. This text synopsis does not supersede the manual's detailed numbered illustrations; direct the installer to the appropriate page before work, and do not invent connector ordering not legible in these paragraphs.

## Plumbing, leveling and starting operation — GE-INSTALL-WATER-001 [DOCUMENTED; pp. 38–43]

**Water connection:** only icemaker/dispenser-equipped models; potable cold water, **25–120 psi**. Before connecting, ensure refrigerator unplugged and supply off. p. 38 details approved tubing, coupling/nut connection and securing/clamping **using existing refrigerator holes only—do not drill into refrigerator**. Check leaks when supply is restored and handle tubing gently when moving the unit. p. 41 begins the separate water-line installation: cold supply and properly accessible shutoff, tubing of suitable length with service loop, compression connectors, careful routing, and flushing before attaching to refrigerator. Subsequent pages show numbered stages (shutoff valve, drilling into house supply if appropriate, fastening, connecting/flush, final hookup). Follow locality plumbing codes and requirements. This PDF does not qualify an inexperienced customer to perform unfamiliar plumbing work.

**Leveling:** pp. 39–40 distinguish leveling feet/rollers and French-door door-alignment adjustment. Refrigerator should sit solidly and doors seal correctly; follow applicable illustrated mechanism. Shelf placements on p. 40 are suggested for best energy efficiency for specific model groups; do not present them as universal fixed shelf positions.

## Normal operating sounds — GE-NORMAL-001 [DOCUMENTED; p. 44]

The manual explicitly considers many sounds normal: high-efficiency compressor **hum/pulsation** and longer/faster operation, **whoosh** when door closes as pressures equalize; variable-speed fan sounds with new power, frequent openings, food loads; **pops/clicks/cracks** while cooling and expanding/contracting in defrost; falling ice cubes; initial movement of water lines during dispensing; coolant gurgling; sizzling, popping, dripping during defrost. Distinguish these from customer-described persistent malfunction; do not assert that *every* noise is harmless solely because a similar word appears in this section.

## Customer symptom routing — GE-TROUBLE-001 [DOCUMENTED; pp. 45–47]

| Customer says / search aliases | Source-supported possible condition and documented first action |
|---|---|
| Fridge will not run; everything off | Defrost may require **about 30 minutes**; verify setting not OFF, power plug fully inserted, home fuse/breaker, and if in showroom mode unplug/replug (p. 45). Do not confuse panel OFF with electrical isolation. |
| Runs too long / repeatedly cycles | New installation: allow **24 hours**; large food load, open doors, hot weather, frequent entry or very cold control selection may explain it (p. 45). |
| Refrigerator or freezer too warm | Select colder setting one step if appropriate; check open door/obstructing food package, frequent door opening; allow stabilization time (p. 45). |
| Freezer cooling but fridge not cooling after startup/outage | Listed normal during initial power-up or extended outage; allow **24 hours** for both compartments (p. 45). Persistent failure after that is not diagnosed by this statement. |
| Frost, ice crystals on packages | Prolonged/frequent open door, or package obstructing closure; some frost within food package is normal (p. 45). |
| Cabinet divider is warm | Automatic anti-condensation/energy-saver design warms outer edge; normal (p. 45). |
| No ice | Check icemaker switch ON, water valve and line, adequate cooling / 24-hour wait, ice pile/feeler arm interference, stuck cubes; switch off before removing jammed cubes (p. 45). |
| Ice tastes/smells bad | Empty and clean bucket; discard stale cubes; seal strong-smelling food and clean cabinet (p. 45). |
| Small/hollow ice | Filter may be clogged: replace cartridge or fit bypass (p. 45). |
| Ice slow to freeze | Check door left open and freezer control setting (p. 45). |
| Frequent icemaker buzz | ON with water not connected: switch **OFF** to avoid water-valve damage (p. 45). |
| First glass warm / water tastes unpleasant | Newly installed refrigeration needs **24 hours**; previously unused water lines need flushing; drained reservoir may need hours to chill (p. 46). |
| Dispenser produces no water | Water shutoff/line, clogged filter, trapped air, or dispenser LOCK; documented responses include water-line check, filter/bypass, dispensing to purge and **3-second Lock** release (p. 46, control type as applicable). |
| Water spurts after new filter | Dispense for **3 minutes (~1.5 gallons)** per this symptom table (p. 46). |
| No water but ice works | Frozen reservoir is listed; **call service**, do not improvise heater methods (p. 46). |
| No water and no ice | Supply/shutoff blockage: call plumber; check filter/bypass and proper filter seating (p. 46). |
| Smell in refrigerator | Cover strong-smelling food, clean interior; open baking-soda box replaced **every 3 months** (p. 46). |
| Moisture outside or interior fog | High humidity and frequent/long door opening may be normal; wipe dry, reduce openings where possible (p. 46). |
| Interior light out | Check electrical supply, breakers; for LED failure call service (p. 46). |
| Warm airflow beneath cabinet | Warm discharge for motor cooling is described as normal; some flooring may discolor (p. 46). |
| Refrigerator never shuts off yet temperatures okay | Adaptive defrost can keep compressor running; manual says it will cycle off after door remains closed for 2 hours (p. 46). |
| Door/drawer does not close itself | Adjust leveling legs as specified (p. 47). Some freezer drawer pop-open when fridge door closes is described as seal effect if drawer closes itself (p. 47). |
| Food freezing in fridge | Move food away from back air vent; adjust refrigerator control **one step warmer** (p. 47). |
| Water drips from internal dispenser | Keep glass in place 2–3 seconds after release; source also notes air in water line (pp. 13, 47). |
| Display says `tc` | TurboCool activated; identify control type and explain mode (p. 47). |
| Filter light stays red | Check installed XWFE (not XWF), correct seating, label condition, reset where supported (p. 47). |
| Red LED/LEAK/error or filter-status beep | See XWFE diagnostic distinction above and p. 47: leak versus failure to detect/filter communication. |
| Handle loose; cabinet doors uneven | Review proper handle attachment and French-door leveling sections, pp. 26–27, 39, 47. |
| Freezer basket jammed | Remove/rearrange food loaded above basket rim; ensure closure (p. 47). |

This table covers documented customer-facing first responses, not internal component-level fault diagnosis. Do not recommend electrical/refrigerant disassembly based on symptom wording.

## Warranty, jurisdictions and support — GE-WARRANTY-001 [DOCUMENTED; pp. 48–49, 52]

For US customers, the refrigerator limited warranty covers **one year** from original purchase for eligible parts defects, with associated labor/service under stated terms. Included water-filter cartridge has a **30-day** defect warranty; separate water-filter limited warranty on p. 49 also states 30 days with limitations. The manual describes **five years** for the sealed refrigerating system on **GE Profile and GE Café** models, not automatically every GE model. Examples of excluded issues: teaching service calls, improper installation/maintenance, abuse/commercial use, food spoilage, household fuses, post-delivery damage, pressure/sediment-related filter issues, consumable replacement, accidents, cosmetic issues not timely reported, misuse of non-GE filters, operation without icemaker water, and normal operation mistaken for a defect. Conditions differ by region; Canada/Québec notes are printed on p. 48. The service technician may use onboard diagnostics port and manual describes an option to request that appliance data not be sent to GE.

**Published customer channels (as printed, not live-verified):** US `GEAppliances.com`, Canada `GEAppliances.ca`; product registration and scheduling/service URLs are on p. 52; US parts `GEApplianceparts.com` / **877.959.8688**; Canada service **800.561.3344**, parts **800.661.1616**; US extended warranty **800.626.2224**, Canada **866.277.9842**. Some printed BODEWELL numbers use alphanumeric digits; consult p. 52 rather than guessing numerical expansion. Proof of purchase is generally needed for warranty claims. Use jurisdiction-specific printed warranty text before asserting coverage.
